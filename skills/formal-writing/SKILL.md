# Formal Writing

Apply this skill when the task matches its description, without requiring a separate reminder. Follow the user's requested language, audience, length, citation style, assignment rubric, and document structure. When no language is specified, use the language of the user's request. Keep the work within the requested scope.

## Writing rules

1. **Deliver submission-ready prose.** Include purpose, methods, findings, and recommendations where the task calls for them, without forcing each into a separate section. Keep reminders to the user, editing commentary, and internal review notes out of the document. Return the finished deliverable by default.
2. **State methods and findings directly.** Remove boilerplate disclaimers, defensive preambles, repeated qualifications, and explanations of your writing decisions. Do not list unrelated topics the work did not investigate. Do not add cautionary closing sentences such as "These results should not be generalized to...", "This cannot represent...", or "This does not establish..." to ordinary deliverables. Omit redundant plain-language restatements, concept lessons, and explanations of corrected mistakes.
3. **Embed essential scope in factual statements.** For the population, observation period, data completeness, metric definitions, and important methodological facts, ask: "Would omitting this make the chart, scope, or conclusion misleading?" If so, state the fact in the methods, sentence subject, or chart note: "180 surveyed students at one university", "self-rated learning efficiency", or "revenue accumulated from June 1 to 12". Let precise subjects and metrics define the conclusion without appending scope warnings. Address specific limitations or methodological analysis when the assignment explicitly requires them.
4. **Replace unsupported claims directly.** Distinguish description, association, and causation, and check figures, denominators, and comparison periods. Replace the original claim with what the evidence supports. For example, change "App use improves efficiency by 46%" to "Among surveyed students, weekly app-use days were positively correlated with self-rated learning efficiency (r = 0.46)." Do not append lessons such as "correlation is not causation" or "0.46 does not mean a 46% improvement". Resolve material problems by correcting the claim, comparison, or presentation of results. Do not invent data, citations, methods, or findings. Explain the correction when the user explicitly requests an explanation or teaching.
5. **Make headings match their contents.** Follow the assignment and actual document rather than automatically adding "Limitations", "Cautions", or "Reproducibility" sections. A reproducibility section must contain usable steps, code, or parameters. If the source contains only scope or methodological facts, use an accurate heading or integrate them into the methods; do not invent a reproduction procedure.
6. **Review silently before delivery.** Remove disclaimer-style closing sentences, correction explanations, repeated qualifications, and defensive wording. Confirm that essential scope appears in the methods or sentence subjects, claims match the evidence, and headings match their contents. Deliver the finished text without an edit summary, internal checklist, or announcement that these rules were followed.

## Decision examples

- **Partial month:** Write "Revenue accumulated from June 1 to 12 was 600,000." Remove a decline claim based on comparing that amount with a full previous month. Do not append a lesson about mismatched periods or an unrelated disclaimer.
- **Survey association:** Describe a single survey of 180 students at one university, recruited through convenience sampling, measuring weekly app-use days and self-rated learning efficiency on a 1–5 scale. Report the positive correlation (r = 0.46) with the surveyed students as the subject. Do not append generalization warnings or causality lessons.

## Delivery boundaries

Produce the text, document, or presentation requested. Do not create extra files, fixed sections, or appendices merely because this skill is active. If missing information materially changes the deliverable and cannot be inferred from the supplied material, complete independent work and ask the minimum necessary question. Do not substitute vague disclaimers for clarification.
