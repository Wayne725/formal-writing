# Formal Writing

Apply this skill when the task matches its description, without requiring a separate reminder. Follow the user's requested language, audience, length, citation style, assignment rubric, and document structure. When no language is specified, use the language of the user's request. Keep the work within the requested scope.

Prefer affirmative wording when it preserves the meaning. Use a concise negative statement when it is the clearest accurate expression of a necessary fact, boundary, or risk. Identify proposed follow-ups and mitigations as recommendations or future work.

## Writing rules

1. **Deliver submission-ready prose.** Include purpose, methods, findings, and recommendations where the task calls for them, without forcing each into a separate section. Keep reminders to the user, editing commentary, and internal review notes out of the document. Return the finished deliverable by default.
2. **State methods and findings affirmatively.** Write what the work did, what it found, and how the result is used. End each section on its last finding or on the step it feeds into. Express scope and decision logic as what the method does ("Customer types are assigned by the combination of R, F, and M scores; the total score supports ranking."), not as what it cannot do. Cover only what the work investigated, and state each point once. Where the document type conventionally includes limitations or risks (thesis, capstone report, proposal), place them once, in that section, as specific statements tied to this study's scope, each paired with a follow-up or mitigation. Present corrections as the corrected claim itself, with no commentary about the original error.
3. **Embed essential scope in factual statements.** For the population, observation period, data completeness, metric definitions, and important methodological facts, ask: "Would omitting this make the chart, scope, or conclusion misleading?" If so, state the fact in the methods, sentence subject, or chart note: "180 surveyed students at one university", "self-rated learning efficiency", or "revenue accumulated from June 1 to 12". Let precise subjects and metrics define the conclusion without appending boilerplate or repetitive scope warnings. Address specific limitations or methodological analysis when the assignment requires them or the document type conventionally includes them.
4. **Replace unsupported claims directly.** Distinguish description, association, and causation, and check figures, denominators, and comparison periods. Replace the original claim with what the evidence supports. For example, change "App use improves efficiency by 46%" to "Among surveyed students, weekly app-use days were positively correlated with self-rated learning efficiency (r = 0.46)." Do not append lessons such as "correlation is not causation" or "0.46 does not mean a 46% improvement". Resolve material problems by correcting the claim, comparison, or presentation of results. Do not invent data, citations, methods, or findings. Explain the correction when the user explicitly requests an explanation or teaching.
5. **Make headings match their contents.** Follow the assignment and actual document rather than automatically adding "Limitations", "Cautions", or "Reproducibility" sections. A reproducibility section must contain usable steps, code, or parameters. If the source contains only scope or methodological facts, use an accurate heading or integrate them into the methods; do not invent a reproduction procedure.
6. **Check silently before delivery.** Confirm that each section's last sentence states a finding or a next step; that essential scope appears in the methods or sentence subjects; that each claim matches its evidence; that headings match their contents; and that limitations appear once, in the conventional place. Deliver the finished text only, without an edit summary, internal checklist, or note that these rules were followed.

## Decision examples

- **Partial month:** Write "Revenue accumulated from June 1 to 12 was 600,000." Remove a decline claim based on comparing that amount with a full previous month. Do not append a lesson about mismatched periods or an unrelated disclaimer.
- **Survey association:** Describe a single survey of 180 students at one university, recruited through convenience sampling, measuring weekly app-use days and self-rated learning efficiency on a 1–5 scale. Report the positive correlation (r = 0.46) with the surveyed students as the subject. Do not append boilerplate generalization warnings or unsolicited causality lessons.

## Delivery boundaries

Produce the text, document, or presentation requested. Do not create extra files, fixed sections, or appendices merely because this skill is active. If missing information materially changes the deliverable and cannot be inferred from the supplied material, complete independent work and ask the minimum necessary question. Do not substitute vague disclaimers for clarification.
