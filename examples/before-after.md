# Before-and-after examples

These examples use fictional data. The revised text illustrates the intended writing style.

## Revenue report

### Request

Rewrite this draft as a submission-ready report. Do not add disclaimers or editing explanations.

### Before

Monthly revenue from January through May 2026 was 1,000,000, 1,100,000, 1,050,000, 1,200,000, and 1,250,000, respectively. Revenue through June 12 was 600,000. June revenue plunged 52% from May, proving that the new marketing strategy failed.

Limitations and reproducibility: June data is incomplete. This report is not investment advice. It does not investigate every competing brand, and its accuracy cannot be guaranteed.

### After

Monthly revenue from January through May 2026 was 1,000,000, 1,100,000, 1,050,000, 1,200,000, and 1,250,000, respectively, showing an overall upward trend. May revenue was 25% higher than January revenue. Revenue accumulated from June 1 to 12 was 600,000.

## Survey analysis

### Request

Rewrite this draft as a submission-ready report. Do not add disclaimers or editing explanations.

### Before

In September 2026, a single online survey using convenience sampling collected 180 valid responses from students at one university. The questionnaire measured weekly learning-app use in days and self-rated learning efficiency on a 1–5 scale. The variables were positively correlated (r = 0.46), proving that using the app improves learning efficiency by 46%. Increasing app use will therefore improve learning outcomes for all university students.

Limitations and cautions: The study used one survey and collected no before-and-after measurements. This report is for reference only, guarantees no outcomes, and does not investigate other learning tools.

### After

In September 2026, a single online survey using convenience sampling collected 180 valid responses from students at one university. The questionnaire measured weekly learning-app use in days and self-rated learning efficiency on a 1–5 scale.

Among surveyed students, weekly app-use days were positively correlated with self-rated learning efficiency (r = 0.46).
